# Route-aware geometric option

The goal is full track completion and skidpad R,R,L,L with zero physical cone contacts. This is an architecture proposal and one measured route-ambiguity check on saved TRAIN evidence. It is not a vision evaluation or a qualified driving candidate. The final multi-seed cases remain unopened.

## Decision

Do not select the existing `GeometricPolicy` or another plan follower as the next driving candidate. First test whether the frozen image cone head supplies a usable local corridor on saved TRAIN images, and whether a nonprivileged lap state can choose the corridor at each crossing. If either fails, further planner tuning cannot establish this route. If both pass, prototype a measured-state feedback controller on that corridor and test its execution separately. This follows the Attack the Premise principle. Three failed actuator fits challenge whether the available visual representation and action mapping provide sufficient route and feedback information. The perfect-future follower contact result also challenges whether the existing follower can execute a reference safely.

The proposed option uses the existing image detector, calibrated projection, short-lived local cone tracks, a route state machine, and a curvature-aware controller. It has no learned action head and requires no new training. This is a testable alternative, not a finding that cone perception or geometric control is adequate. A directly learned local corridor remains a fallback only if the image cone head fails the detection gate. No such corridor model is currently shown to exist or have a qualified data contract.

## Evidence and current boundaries

`src/fsop/eval/geometric.py` pairs each blue cone with the nearest yellow cone and greedily orders the midpoints by distance from the previous point. Its nominal 1.75 m one-sided offset is in the ego lateral direction, rather than a normal to a curved boundary. Pairing can cross lanes at the skidpad intersection, and greedy order has no route or curvature continuity. Missing cones produce a straight plan at at least 2 m/s. The current plan also stops its positions at the final visible point while retaining a nonzero target speed.

`src/fsop/eval/closed_loop.py` ignores cone scores when passing detections to the planner. It never passes `obs.desire` to `plan_from_cones`. Its default model source prefers the image head when present and projects the bottom of each detection onto a flat ground plane using `env.T_ego_cam()`. `src/fsop/eval/img_to_bev.py` uses per-class 0.6 m merging and accepts ground rays down to a near-horizon cutoff, so distance, mount error, road slope, calibration, duplicated detections, missed cones, and swapped colors all matter. The learned BEV head is an alternate source, not evidence that either head meets the geometric gate.

`RouteCommands.for_mission` and `advance` in `src/fsop/sim/route_commands.py` generate one-frame turn pulses using `Mission` arc length. `Mission.update_progress` disambiguates overlaps with a privileged centerline and a previous-index window. Those are useful simulated inputs but do not show an implementable on-car route state. `mission_brake` similarly uses exact mission progress and must be replaced by a separately evidenced finish and stop mechanism. Do not claim deployability while reading `env.s`, the mission centerline, ground-truth pose, referee events, or simulator cone arrays at runtime.

The clean source at `.work/lab/native_lateral_reference_executed/source_snapshot` supplies a concrete ambiguity check. At 12.70 s and 22.45 s, the saved vehicle positions differ by 0.107 m and yaws by 0.082 rad. The speeds are 5.94 and 6.04 m/s, but the native teacher steering commands are -0.169 and -0.020 rad. The first point is near the end of right lap one, and the second is near the right-to-left transition, according to the recorded crossing times in `run1/comparison.json`. These are near poses, not identical camera frames, and the commands are observations rather than a proof of camera ambiguity. They demonstrate why a stateless local pose or nearest-segment rule is inadequate. The check reads TRAIN teacher states and controls only and is not a perception result. Run `python3 .work/lab/next_strategy/geometric_option_probe/route_ambiguity.py` from the worktree to reproduce it. The script verifies the saved state and control hashes before printing its result.

`NativeExpert` uses measured vehicle state plus privileged `Mission` geometry and progress. The recorded zero-contact teacher therefore validates neither a vision controller nor the existing plan follower. The native lateral reference comparison found contacts for the followers despite using a privileged recorded future. The proposed controller must earn an independent physical execution gate.

The local worktree lacks `data/native/lab_v7/manifest.json`, `probes/pi_models/latest_pi_train.pt`, and `artifacts/models/prep`. The clean teacher source snapshot does not contain camera arrays or predicted cones. Its `cones.npy` is ground truth. However, `runs/direct_command_pilot3/evidence` and `runs/direct_command_recovery_pilot2/evidence` contain saved image-head outputs and the same-call decoded cone predictions for a TRAIN case. They are failed candidate-driven pilots, not teacher laps. Both use the same skidpad seed and light matched appearance. Neither covers a full lap or an independent seed.

The rerunnable `PYTHONPATH=src:. python3 .work/lab/next_strategy/geometric_option_probe/cone_quality.py` checks evidence frame hashes and restricts each pilot to frames before its first reported physical contact. The scorer alone receives actual cone positions and class labels. It matches blue and yellow predictions to any actual blue or yellow cone within 0.75 m, then checks color. Within 5 to 10 m, the first pilot has 203 matched predictions out of 207 and the recovery pilot has 151 out of 153. Within 10 to 20 m, the respective counts are 380 of 662 and 368 of 636. Within 0 to 5 m, they are 16 of 30 and 10 of 20. Frame samples are correlated, and two pilots of one seed are not two independent scenes. Frustum-based visible-GT recall at 5 to 10 m is 196 of 197 and 145 of 147, but frustum inclusion does not account for occlusion. Matched-error 95th percentiles at 5 to 10 m are 0.245 m and 0.248 m; the 0.75 m match gate excludes larger errors and cannot certify clearance. This evidence supports testing a short-range corridor with temporal tracking, but outliers and near-field visibility remain substantial blockers. It cannot establish full-route feasibility or physical safety. The probe did not run a model, simulator, GPU, or final case.

## Smallest deployable design to test

At each camera tick, take both camera images, the calibrated camera mount, body-frame longitudinal and lateral speed, yaw rate, measured steering where available, and a monotonic timestamp. Freeze detector weights and all preprocessing. Decode image-head predictions as `(x, y, class, score)` in the current ego frame. Retain unknown-color detections as possible obstacles. Project detections only within a validated range and reject nonfinite, below-ground-invalid, near-horizon, and physically implausible results. A confidence score is a ranking signal until calibration evidence supports a bound. A high score does not by itself mean a cone is safe to ignore.

Use measured velocity and yaw rate to transport short-lived cone tracks between frames. Associate with spatial, class, and motion-consistency gates. Carry uncertainty and time since observation. Delete stale tracks after an explicitly frozen time and grow uncertainty during occlusion. Treat conflicting colors as uncertain rather than averaging them into a confident boundary. Do not use the recorded world pose or exact `cones.npy` as a planner input. The prototype should also emit an untracked single-frame result so that a mapping error cannot hide behind smoothing.

Build a small set of continuous corridor hypotheses from blue and yellow boundaries and uncolored obstacle exclusions. Candidate edges must respect cone spacing, plausible local width, heading, curvature, clearance for the full vehicle footprint, and continuity with the last accepted path. Use a bounded local search, not global nearest-neighbor pairing. A one-sided estimate is allowed only if prior measured corridor width and tangent are both sufficiently recent and their uncertainty still leaves the footprint clear. At an intersection, route state chooses among feasible hypotheses. If there is no unique safe hypothesis, do not use the old straight-line fallback. Emit an explicit `unknown` status, limit speed based on observable stopping distance, and brake or stop before entering the unobserved region. This behavior must be physically tested. A stop can avoid contact but does not satisfy mission completion.

Implement route state as an explicit sequence `entry, right_1, right_2, left_1, left_2, exit, stop`, with unknown or invalid as a separate state. Use detected gate geometry, odometry, and turn winding or other observable passage evidence with direction and hysteresis. A crossing event may advance at most once. Duplicate detections, reverse crossings, skipped gates, reset, odometry drift, and contradictory evidence must not advance the counter silently. Trackdrive needs its own lap counter and finish detection. The current pulse interface alone does not establish the counter, because the simulation generates it from privileged progress. Treat recorded pulses as an externally supplied oracle in an ablation only, not as proof that the state machine works on-car.

For control, test the existing `Expert`-style feedback on the selected local corridor using measured `vx`, `vy`, yaw rate, and steering, with curvature preview and a conservative speed constrained by curvature, visibility, clearance, braking, and actuator limits. The exact controller, reference point, steering sign, measured-state interface, speed target, latency, and finish policy must be frozen before evaluation. Do not assume a pure-pursuit or OP plan follower is safe because the native teacher is safe. The teacher's feedback and privileged geometry differ from a fixed-clock plan follower.

## Cheap decisive TRAIN-only experiment

This experiment needs no simulator, GPU, or training. Start with the recorded detector outputs in the two local TRAIN pilots. The full clean teacher lap still needs its saved TRAIN images and pinned checkpoint on the host that contains them. Do not fetch final cases. Before running, freeze the episode list, source and checkpoint hashes, image preprocessing, detector, route inputs, thresholds, and report schema. Include the complete clean skidpad teacher episode `train-skidpad-82000-matched`, at least one matched TRAIN trackdrive episode, and the two independently qualified recovery prefixes only if their full input contract and receipts are available. Report each episode and phase separately. A missing phase is a failed coverage gate, not a zero-error sample.

1. Verify TRAIN membership, metadata and array hashes. Preserve camera-frame indices and the saved state and teacher-control ticks. For the local pilots, use saved `prediction.cones_xy`, `cones_cls`, and `cones_score` as already-decoded image-head predictions and preserve the recorded raw image-head outputs for an independent decoding check. For the clean teacher dataset, run the pinned frozen detector on both camera frames and record every detection, its score, and source camera. `NativeEpisode.frame_pair` supplies saved 5 Hz frames as the current and previous model inputs. These are 0.2 s apart, matching the frame skip at 20 Hz for phase zero, but do not recover the missing three intermediate frames or certify the full 20 Hz runtime. Use `eval` mode, CPU FP32, and the same pinned mount and preprocessing as deployment. Run a repeated subset for determinism and compare its outputs. Do not use future images.
2. Feed only predictions, current measured state, timestamps, and recorded or reconstructed past route inputs into the planner. Use the 5 Hz data as an open-loop feasibility test, not a 20 Hz closed-loop replay. To test route selection separately, compare a recorded-command oracle arm with a state-machine arm that derives gate crossings from predicted cones and integrated body motion. The pooled `desires[k]` are `[25,8]` histories, not the original 100 raw 20 Hz commands. Do not reconstruct or claim the missing raw pulse times. Explicitly label any supplied route phase from teacher geometry as an oracle and exclude it from the deployability gate.
3. Keep `cones.npy`, `track.json`, recorded world poses, and future teacher positions in a scorer isolated from the planner. Transform GT cones into each recorded ego pose for scoring only. Include the actual detector's false positives, false negatives, class confusion, range-dependent errors, near-field missed obstacles, and temporal dropout. Match detections to labels with fixed distance gates and report score-binned precision, recall, position error, color confusion, and unmatched counts by distance, phase, and episode. The 0.75 m matching gate in `BevScorer` is a diagnostic, not a safety tolerance. Near-field collisions can matter well below it. Independently audit visibility and occlusion before treating every unseen label as a miss.
4. At every sampled tick with a still-moving teacher, emit a route state, selected corridor, risk status, available length, uncertainty, and proposed controls. Score coverage against all such ticks, including empty or invalid detections. Report earliest unsafe or unknown tick, not only mean error. Score the predicted swept vehicle footprint against every GT cone over the candidate's declared horizon, with the cone radius and explicit uncertainty margin. Also measure the lateral and heading error against the time-aligned recorded teacher continuation, separately from obstacle clearance. This is a geometric diagnostic on teacher states, not a dynamics rollout or a physical contact count. A candidate can differ from the teacher while remaining safe, and an aligned reference can still be untrackable.
5. Specifically inspect entry, both right laps, the right-to-left transition, both left laps, exit, and the trackdrive bends. Log each state transition and gate observation. Repeat the near-pose pair above. The state-machine arm must choose the correct next maneuver at both points without privileged pose, mission progress, exact cone geometry, future data, or teacher labels. Inject a duplicate crossing, a reversed crossing, missing detections, a high-confidence false cone, color swaps, and a brief frame dropout into recorded predictions. Record the exact interventions and require bounded, explicit uncertainty or stop behavior instead of an incorrect confident maneuver.

The prespecified feasibility gate is all of the following. Every required TRAIN episode and phase is present. Every sampled moving tick returns either a finite corridor with strictly positive GT-scored footprint clearance after the frozen margin, or a correctly declared unknown and braking demand. There are zero confident wrong-route selections, zero confident footprint intersections, and no increase of requested speed into an unknown corridor. The nonprivileged state machine must produce the complete correct R,R,L,L and exit sequence without duplicate or missing events. Report the fraction and longest duration of unknown states and the remaining observable stopping distance; a system that avoids false confidence by staying unknown does not pass the mission-feasibility gate. For a preliminary go decision, require that each unknown interval ends with recovery or a stop before the end of the verified free corridor. Compute required stopping distance from recorded speed and the frozen delay and braking assumptions. Freeze the margin and braking assumptions before seeing predictions and show how they account for detector range error, odometry drift, actuation delay, and footprint. If those bounds are not supported, mark the result inconclusive instead of choosing an attractive margin after inspection.

The gate is deliberately stringent for selecting an approach and does not assert that passing it proves collision-free operation. A failure identifies whether detection, route state, corridor generation, or actuator feedback needs work. A pass permits a separate closed-loop TRAIN pilot chosen by the parent. It does not open or alter validation or the frozen final acceptance suite.

## Qualification after feasibility

First validate timing and parity on the actual 20 Hz deployment runtime with both cameras, the same sensor calibration, observable route state, CPU backend, and captured detections. Measure and report latency separately from simulated time. Check synchronization and missing data behavior. Exercise recovery, stale-map and stop behavior. The 5 Hz dataset cannot establish those properties.

Then require independent native physical execution under the existing TRAIN and validation gates. Require the actual route, complete finish and stop, zero physical contacts, zero off-course events, and valid recovery across the prescribed conditions. Audit the action provenance, route events, exact detector outputs, physical contact stream, and state-machine transitions. Verify stop behavior and no hidden mission geometry access. Do not substitute the geometric footprint scorer, pointwise teacher-control error, a visual dependence ablation, or a single seed for this physical gate. Keep the already frozen multi-seed final acceptance untouched until the parent selects a candidate that passes all earlier gates.
